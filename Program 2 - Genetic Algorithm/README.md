AI Usage

1. What is the initial population size?: 120
2. How do you evaluate an individual?: The algorithm starts by separating each key in either the chromosome being checked or the target chromosome into one of 3 camps: a target key that isn't in checked chromosome (missing_keys), a checked chromosome key that isn't in target (extra_keys), and keys in both. If the chromosomes have a different amount of keys, 1000 is added to the fitness. The algorithm then makes a sorted list of all combinations of missing and extra keys based on the Levenshtein distance (the amount of mutations to the checked key needed for it to match the target key). It turns that sorted list into the list of pairs of missing and extra keys, where each key is only used at most once, that has the least total Levenshtein distance. That least total Levenshtein distance is added to the fitness. That list is called paired_keys. The keys the two chromosomes have matching are added to paired_keys as pairs. The algorithm then runs through paired_keys, getting the values of the checked chromosome and target. If the values are both strings: their Levenshtein distance is added to the fitness score. If theyre both integers: the absolute value of their difference divided by 30 and then rounded up is added to the fitness. If theyre different types then 1000 is added to the fitness. The total fitness is then returned.
3. What are the stopping criteria?: The GA loop (evolve_match function) stops when a chromosome with fitness = 0 is found or run time is over 170 seconds.
4. How do you select the chromosomes?: I randomly take half the population(60 chromosomes), sort them by fitness, and take the top half of that random sorted selection..
5. How often do you perform crossover and mutation? Are the crossover and mutation rates fixed or adaptive?: Crossover is performed every generation, taking the 30 or so tournament winners and randomly creating population_size/2 offsprings. Those offspring and another population_size/2-10 offspring who aren't crossovered then go through mutation where theres a fixed 10% chance they add a key and 10% chance they delete a key. Theres an adaptive mutation rate for the keys and values, averaging 1 mutations to the keys and values combined per offspring. The 5 best individuals are sent to the next round along with 5 mutated forms of the best individuals.
